U12 · Understanding layer · 17 Aug 2026

bge-m3 beat the incumbent on every family. Then I checked whether the reason was real.

A bake-off against the live Workers AI embedder: 31 projects, 200 queries, 120 real negatives, three arms. m3 scored a clean sweep. The single family driving that sweep turned out to describe nobody — so this report throws it out and re-decides on what is left.

Verdict: adopt bge-m3 — for a smaller and better-evidenced reason than the headline.

Not because of multilingual retrieval. Because at a matched operating point it false-binds on 0.8% of real negative turns where the deployed model binds 9.2% — an eleven-fold cut in the bot confidently naming a project nobody asked about.

Cost is a full re-index: 1024 dims against 768. That is why this had to be decided before U9 finishes calibrating bands, not after.

What ran

Three arms against the real @cf/baai embedder through a local wrangler dev --remote worker, so no token passed through the harness. Scoring is accuracy@1 and margin — never raw cosine, because the two models put scores on different scales and an absolute threshold tuned on one says nothing about the other.

margin = (top1 − top2) / top1, separation inside a single model's own ranking.

bge-base is documented to want a query prefix; m3 is not. Production passes raw text at every call site, so bge-base runs both ways — as-deployed and best-practice — and m3 has to beat the better of the two. Handicapping the incumbent would have made the result worthless.

Read naively this is a rout, and translit — project names written in Devanagari and Kannada — is three-quarters of the gap. That is exactly the multilingual claim bge-m3 is bought for. Which is why it needed checking rather than celebrating.

Two things I checked before believing it

Correction — my first read of the negatives was backwards

m3's 90th-percentile margin on negatives is higher (0.119 vs 0.068), and I initially wrote that up as m3 being more prone to confident false binds. That was a scale artifact: m3 shifts the whole distribution up, positives and negatives together. Compared at a matched operating point — a threshold admitting the same share of true binds — the ordering reverses completely, and m3 is far safer. Raw percentile comparisons across models are the same mistake as comparing raw cosine.

Struck — the family that drove the headline describes nobody

If translit is going to justify a re-index, buyers have to actually type that way. They do not:

  • corpus/recovered-raw.jsonl — 13,555 rows, zero Devanagari, zero Kannada. The 4,119 rows tagged hi-en are Hinglish in Latin script ("konsa better", "kaun better hai") — that is the hinglish family, where bge-base already scores 0.968.
  • turn_ledger on dev — 43,530 real turns. 512 carry non-ASCII, but sampling 400 shows 333 are em-dashes and ₹ signs, 26 emoji. Only 24 are Indic script.
  • Pulling every Indic turn: 46 rows, and all of them are our own test traffic. The top phone is literally +9190stress2026; the rest are sequential generated numbers. Zero organic Indic buyers.

The family also tests the wrong shape. Of the 12 Indic turns that mix in Latin text, the pattern is the reverse of what I built: the project name stays in Latin and the question is Indic — ಒಂದು ಪ್ರಶ್ನೆ: brochure ಕಳುಹಿಸಿ. That loads the intent lane, not name retrieval, which is what this harness scores.

So translit is struck from the verdict. It stays in the harness — the day a tenant serves buyers who type that way, the measurement is already built — but it cannot count toward a decision until such a buyer exists.

What survives

Same run, translit removed: 186 queries across the five families that reflect observed behaviour, scored against the 120 real ledger negatives. The threshold column is set per-arm to admit 95% of that arm's true binds, which is what makes the false-bind rates comparable.

The accuracy gap narrows from 10 points to 4.8 — and six of bge-base's nine remaining misses are a single family, typo. That one is not in doubt: buyers misspell constantly, and a typo is already known to eat the topic, not just the name.

The false-bind column is the finding that does not depend on anything I constructed.

Calibration guidance for U9

Chasing 99% recall is not available at any price: on a 31-document pool the threshold falls below the noise and every model floods. Put the auto-bind band at 90%, where m3 gives 0.8% against the incumbent's 9.2%, and let the reranker and ask-back lanes carry the last ten percent — which is what U10 is for.

What m3 still gets wrong, and why it is not the embedder's problem

The four negatives m3 binds at its 90% threshold are one category — turns carrying real intent but no project name at all:

0.169"force all same day Monday"→ Brigade Atmosphere
0.166"okay i would like to visit this too"→ Desire Spaces
0.153"is EC clear?"→ HV Echo Twin
0.151"I want to visit"→ Desire Spaces

bge-base fails on exactly the same category — "2 BHK configs", "my visits?", "Floor plan?" — just far more often. A buyer saying "I want to visit" already has an active project in session; resolving a name there is the band's job and the session's, not the embedder's. Swapping models narrows this failure but does not remove it, and nothing in this report should be read as claiming otherwise.

On near-identical catalog neighbours — Brigade Cornerstone against Brigade Cornerstone Utopia, Krishnaja Greens against Viva Greens — both bge-base arms miss 4 of 28. m3 misses none.

The prefix arm: declined

Adding bge-base's documented query prefix looked like a free win — no migration, no re-index, +1 point of accuracy and a visibly fatter margin. It is not free.

The prefix inflates margins on negatives too, so it buys one point of accuracy and pays nearly six points of false-bind. Left off. This is the same trap as the raw-percentile reading above: a fatter margin is only good news if the negatives did not fatten with it.

What it costs

Latency is not the objection — measured through the same proxy, p50 over repeated calls:

The real price is the dimension change. 1024 against 768 means:

  • New Vectorize indexes for both PROJECT_VECTORS and INTENT_VECTORS — dimensions are fixed at creation, so neither can be migrated in place.
  • A full re-embed of both corpora: 431 project vectors and the 13.5k intent corpus.
  • Refitting the learned projection p256-f6665e0b79, a 256×768 matrix fitted to the current space. It becomes 256×1024 and must be retrained, not reshaped.

What this changes about the plan

Sequencing — U12 has to land before U9 completes

U9's job is calibrating the margin bands. Between the two models the mean margin moves 0.200 → 0.330 and the 95% threshold moves 0.052 → 0.150 — roughly 2.9×. Every band calibrated in bge-base space is thrown away by a cutover. Calibrate once, in the space we are keeping.

  • U9 — hold the BANDS.rrf calibration until the cutover lands, then calibrate in m3 space at the 90% operating point above.
  • U11 — auto-teaching project name variants writes into the index, so it follows the cutover rather than preceding it. Teaching into a space we are about to discard is wasted work.
  • U10 — unaffected. The reranker consumes candidates, not a fixed embedding space.

What this evidence is not

  • The positives are constructed. The ledger cannot supply ground truth here — it records also tell me about krishnaja greens as binding ayana, a known wrong bind. All three arms face identical constructions, so the comparison is fair; the absolute numbers are not a field measurement.
  • The negatives are real, and they carry the finding that matters. That asymmetry is deliberate.
  • The pool is 31 documents. That is not a shortcut — retrieval is namespaced per builder, so ~31 is the true operating pool for this tenant. It does mean a perfect score is a weaker claim than it looks; m3 is not perfect, it is unbeaten on 186 constructed queries.
  • One tenant, one day. Nothing here has been measured on prod traffic.
